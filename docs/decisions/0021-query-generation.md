# 0021: How the 200 benchmark queries are generated, curated and split

- Status: accepted for the method (author, 2026-11-21); the grid in this record is proposed and waits for the
  author's approval before anything is generated
- Date: 2026-11-21

## Context

The benchmark rules (specification Section 9) fix the target: 200 queries, 50 per type (simple keyword,
entity-focused, concept-focused, complex natural language), 40 in Hindi or Kannada (20 each), about 40 in
real question styles (exam, tourist, researcher), generated by an LLM from descriptions of who is searching
and why, never from the seed entity list or from system output, and curated by the author. The share of
queries with a linkable entity is reported, and the queries are split 40 dev / 160 test.

This record settles how the descriptions are made, which model writes them, how Hindi and Kannada queries are
made, and what happens to a query with no relevant document.

Comparable work: UQV100 (Bailey et al., SIGIR 2016) had people write queries from short backstories; Alaofi
et al. (SIGIR 2023) asked whether LLMs can generate query variants from such backstories and compared them
with people's; TREC Deep Learning 2023 mixed synthetic queries into a judged test collection and reported how
they behaved. TREC drops topics with no relevant document from the metrics and reports the count.

## Options considered

Where the information-need descriptions (backstories) come from:

| Option | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. A fixed grid (role × purpose × heritage area × region × period), one LLM-written backstory per sampled cell, curated by the author | Even coverage; scales; no entity names go in; every cell reproducible from a seed | Backstories are LLM-written; disclosed |
| B. The author writes about 200 backstories | Most realistic | Slow; the author's knowledge of the corpus may tilt them |
| C. A for about 160; the author writes the about 40 real-style ones | Real styles by a person | The author's tilt on 40 queries |

Generator model:

| Option | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. A short trial of 2–3 open-weight models, the author rating Hindi and Kannada fluency | Free; checks Kannada before committing | A day of Colab work |
| B. One open model chosen now | Fastest | Kannada quality unknown until curation |
| C. A paid API model | Strongest in Hindi and Kannada | Costs money; breaks the free-first rule |

Hindi and Kannada queries:

| Option | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Generated directly in each language from their own backstories; the author curates both | Natural queries; RQ3 tested on real Hindi and Kannada needs | Depends on the generator's fluency (the trial checks it) |
| B. English queries translated by a fluent person | Controlled | Less natural; overlaps with the translate-then-search control |

Queries with no relevant document after judging:

| Option | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Left out of the metrics and counted per type and language, as TREC does | Standard; no noise from all-zero queries | Fewer than 200 queries scored |
| B. Every query scored | All 200 | Adds noise to every comparison |
| C. Replaced from a reserve after judging | Keeps 200 | Replacing after judging looks like selection on outcomes |

## Decision

Backstories A; generator A; Hindi and Kannada A, the author curating both languages; no-relevant rule A,
written into `docs/plan/analysis_plan.md` before any test-set run.

## Proposed grid (waiting for the author's approval)

The generator sees only one grid cell and the target query type, language and style. It never sees the
entity lists, the corpus, the gazetteer or any system output. Regions and periods are given as zones and date
ranges, not names, so no entity enters through the grid.

Searcher roles, each with the purposes that fit it:

| Role | Purposes |
| --- | --- |
| School student | Homework; a class project |
| Civil-services or state exam aspirant | Revise art-and-culture facts; check a fact for an exam answer |
| Undergraduate (history, architecture, fine arts) | Write an essay; compare two traditions or styles |
| Researcher (historian, art historian, epigraphist) | Find primary sources; trace how something changed over time |
| Domestic tourist or pilgrim | Plan a visit; learn what to look for at a place |
| Foreign visitor | Understand a place or practice before a trip |
| Museum curator or collector | Identify, date or place an object; find comparable objects |
| Teacher | Prepare a lesson |
| Performer or craftsperson | Learn the origins and lineage of their form |
| Journalist or writer | Background for a feature |
| Local heritage enthusiast | Learn the history of their home area |

Heritage areas (from the six document types, specification Section 2): temples and religious architecture;
forts, palaces and civic architecture; sculpture and bronzes; painting and manuscripts; inscriptions and
coins; textiles, crafts and decorative arts; music; dance and theatre; festivals and rituals; rulers,
dynasties and patrons.

Regions: north, south, east, west, central, north-east, pan-India.

Periods: before 600 CE; 600–1200; 1200–1550; 1550–1800; 1800–1947; living tradition (no period).

Query types: simple keyword (two to four words); entity-focused (names one specific site, object, person,
dynasty or practice); concept-focused (a theme without naming a specific entity); complex natural language
(a question with two or more constraints). About 40 queries carry a real-style flag (exam, tourist,
researcher wording) across the four types.

Quotas and sampling:

| | Simple | Entity | Concept | Complex | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| English | 40 | 40 | 40 | 40 | 160 |
| Hindi | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 20 |
| Kannada | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 20 |

- 200 slots (type × language × real-style flag) are filled from cells sampled with a fixed seed, stratified
  so that each heritage area, region and role appears about equally often. Each slot has two reserve cells
  in a fixed order; a reserve is used only when the author rejects a backstory or query at curation, which
  happens before any system runs or any judging.
- Every backstory and query is saved with the model name and version, prompt, date and cell.
- Curation log (`data/queries/curation_v1.csv`): original text, action (keep, edit or reject), edited text and
  reason, for every item.
- Linkable-entity share: each query is matched against the gazetteer (`data/gazetteer/gazetteer_v1.jsonl.gz`)
  and the share is reported per type and language.
- Split: stratified by type and language with a fixed seed, 40 dev (10 per type, 4 Hindi and 4 Kannada) and
  160 test. The test file's SHA-256 is recorded when it is written; no system is run on it until the analysis
  plan is committed.

## Generator trial

The same 30 cells (10 English, 10 Hindi, 10 Kannada, across the four types) go to each candidate:
Qwen3-14B and Sarvam-M (24B, built on Mistral Small 3.1, post-trained on Indian languages), both Apache-2.0
on their model cards, run in 4-bit with greedy decoding and thinking mode off.

Change of candidates (author, 21 Nov 2026): the first list (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B) was of older
model generations and was replaced before any output was rated. Gemma is left out because Gemma 4 is the
relevance judge (decision 0022). Sarvam-30B was considered, but its official weights are a 129 GB download,
too large for free Colab or Kaggle machines, and the smaller copies are unofficial uploads with no licence. The author rates
each output blind to the model: fluency (1–3), fits the backstory (yes or no), and is the requested type (yes or no).
The model with the best Hindi and Kannada ratings is used. If none produces fluent Kannada, the choice comes
back to the author. Ratings and outputs are saved in `results/queries/generator_trial_v1/`.

## Consequences

- The LLM judge (separate options comparison) must come from a different model family from the generator,
  so the judge does not favour its own phrasing.
- Disclosure (README, data card, paper): backstories and queries LLM-generated from a fixed grid and
  author-curated, with the curation counts.
- The author's knowledge of the corpus still enters through curation; edits are logged so it can be
  measured.
